WikiPulse watches Wikipedia's live edit firehose and flags **spikes** — a wiki
suddenly being edited far more than usual, often the first sign of breaking news
— then serves it on a live dashboard. It's a weekend-sized project with a
deliberate focus: the parts of streaming that actually bite in production, not
clever ML.

## The pipeline

![pipeline](/projects/wikipulse-arch.svg)

Wikipedia's EventStreams (SSE, no auth) are produced to a Kafka topic keyed by
wiki, processed with Quix Streams in tumbling 60-second windows, and written to
DuckDB (or MotherDuck) through an idempotent sink. A Streamlit dashboard reads
the edit-rate and spike tables live. Redpanda stands in for Kafka in dev — the
*exact same client code* runs against Confluent Cloud, only `.env` changes.

## Why the spike model is deliberately dumb

The "model" is mean + k·σ over a rolling baseline, and that's a choice, not a
shortcut. A plain fixed threshold would false-alarm constantly on wikis that
are *always* busy (Wikidata); measuring deviation from each wiki's own baseline
correctly treats sustained busyness as normal and only flags genuine
departures.

The interesting engineering was never the detector — it's windowing,
watermark-based window close, handling late events, and making the sink
idempotent (`INSERT OR REPLACE`) so a replay doesn't double-count. Those are the
things a streaming job gets wrong in production, so those are the things the
project is built to get right.
