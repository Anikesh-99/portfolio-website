Most home-grown backtests flatter themselves. The two usual culprits are
look-ahead bias and ignored costs, so this engine makes both structural
rather than a matter of discipline.

## Event-driven, so the future doesn't exist

Everything flows through four events. A strategy reads only bars up to the
current one because future bars literally aren't reachable, and a signal
generated on bar *t*'s close fills at bar *t+1*'s open, never the close that
triggered it.

![event flow](/projects/event-flow.svg)

There's a unit test that pins the no-look-ahead rule down: it asserts the fill
price equals the next bar's open, so a regression that reintroduces
look-ahead fails the suite.

## Costs and honest parameters

Every fill pays commission and crosses the spread through a slippage model
(buys fill slightly above the open, sells slightly below). Parameter selection
is walk-forward: moving-average windows are chosen on a train slice and scored
once on a held-out slice, next to a buy-and-hold benchmark.
